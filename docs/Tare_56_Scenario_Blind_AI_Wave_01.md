# Tare 56-Scenario Bank — Blind AI Wave 01

## Status

Eight fictional AI testers completed one 30-decision run each against the Version 19, 56-scenario bank. This is a diagnostic wave, not psychometric validation and not the planned three-run sample.

The testers received only ordinary player-visible material: encounter narration, available inquiry prompts, inquiry answers they purchased, Ledger text they chose to consult, ruling options, consequences, decision number, Inquiry Marks, and the normal closing. They did not receive source files, signals, scores, intended constructs, topic tags, adaptive-selection data, admin access, or hidden reports.

The production site's account gate and the isolated preview's browser transport prevented direct automated UI entry. A blind player terminal was therefore used as an access layer for the exact Version 19 bank and route behavior. It enforced the same 60 Inquiry Marks, paid inquiries, required free Decision 16 Ledger event, and 30-decision run. Testers were prohibited from inspecting the terminal's implementation.

## Strongest question-bank findings

### 1. The new opening pools produce useful disagreement

Each run received one scenario from 51–53 and one from 54–56.

- **51 — The Mark That Remains:** the Strict Textualist and Procedural Purist removed the continuing mark after completion of punishment; the Cautious Investigator, Compassionate Consequentialist, and Defensive Minimizer ended its restrictions but preserved a notation. This is meaningful accountability-versus-restoration separation.
- **53 — The Second Uniform:** the three exposed testers all selected supervised reinstatement. The ruling may be a compelling compromise or an overly attractive safe middle and deserves another matched exposure.
- **54 — The Promised Dose:** both exposed testers honored the purchase and promise by delivering the full dose. More philosophically varied exposure is needed before judging the question.
- **55 — The Sister's Record:** three testers chose bounded self-report backed by Tali; the Strict Textualist required immediate disclosure and audit. This usefully separated loyalty-bounded accountability from record-integrity formalism.
- **56 — The Last Household:** both exposed testers assigned the home to Aven because her danger was immediate and irreversible. The Defensive Minimizer treated this as the one case where delay or transfer was less safe.
- **52 — The Restitution Refused:** no tester received this scenario in the first eight seeded routes. It remains untested.

No newly added scenario was marked as a forced choice in this wave.

### 2. Three older scenarios recur as forced-choice problems

- **8 — The Physician's Triage:** flagged by the Authority Skeptic, Cautious Investigator, and Context-Dependent Pragmatist. All three wanted the missing quantitative effect of dividing the medicine to be reviewable before choosing among clinical allocation, equal division, and patient control.
- **31 — The Unstable Weight:** flagged by the Compassionate Consequentialist, Context-Dependent Pragmatist, and Procedural Purist. They wanted some form of containment, inspection, repair, or review that did not irreversibly alter evidence, disable the Station, or permanently refuse the claim.
- **2 — The Forgiven Debt:** flagged by the Cautious Investigator and Defensive Minimizer. The partial-repayment compromise was not supported by either party's agreement, while neither disputed extreme was sufficiently established.

Single-test flags occurred for scenarios 4, 6, 22, 33, and 45. Those should be monitored, not revised from one observation.

### 3. The safest-option tester found a serious safe-middle signal

The Defensive Minimizer judged the most reviewable and personally defensible ruling plainly attractive in 29 of 30 decisions. Its choices repeatedly favored delay, delegation, recusal, supervision, higher review, provisional restriction, or preservation of records.

The costs of those rulings were real but often shifted away from the decision-maker: delayed claims, frozen property, lost information, reduced staffing, transferred responsibility, and burdens imposed on travelers. This suggests the bank should be audited for options that sound prudent because they defer commitment without making their human cost equally vivid.

This is not proof that 29 questions have a dominant answer. The tester was explicitly constructed to seek defensive choices, and this is one run. The useful finding is that the strategy rarely encountered a dilemma where institutional safety itself carried a clearly unavoidable moral cost.

### 4. Inquiry behavior remains highly discriminating

The Strict Textualist retained 36 marks and the Defensive Minimizer retained 31. Five testers finished with three or fewer, and four exhausted the reserve entirely. The question economy therefore continues to elicit visibly different information strategies.

### 5. The ordinary player receives no interpretive result

Every tester saw only the atmospheric closing. The detailed assessment interpretation is admin-only. Multiple testers independently wanted a player-facing recap of observable behavior—such as marks spent, Ledger use, reopened rulings, and forced compromises—without hidden scores or trait labels.

## Recommended next actions

1. Force Scenario 52 into the next matched wave so every new question receives exposure.
2. Give scenarios 53, 54, and 56 to contrasting testers that did not see them in this wave.
3. Audit scenarios 8 and 31 first; each produced the same structural complaint from three different constitutions.
4. Audit the cost framing of delay, delegation, supervision, and review options across the bank, using the Defensive Minimizer trace as the index.
5. Do not revise new scenarios 51 or 55 yet; their initial divergence is desirable.
6. Run two more blind seeds per constitution before treating any pattern as recurring.

## Interpretation limit

These are adversarial narrative tests by AI systems. They can expose ambiguity, dominant compromises, missing information, option bundling, resource behavior, and report-design problems. They cannot establish reliability, validity, fairness, job relevance, or human moral traits.
