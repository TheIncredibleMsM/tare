# Tare — 50-Scenario Master Editorial Bank

## Global mechanics

- Each assessment serves exactly **10 principal encounters** and targets **15–20 minutes**.
- The player begins with **20 Inquiry Marks**. Asking an optional question or voluntarily consulting the Ledger costs one mark. Marks never replenish.
- Automatic Ledger objections cost nothing. Rulings remain available at zero marks.
- Inquiry responses may reveal facts, ambiguity, emotion, refusal, contradiction, or uncertainty; they need not provide definitive facts.
- Every form is balanced across construct families. Dependencies always follow prerequisites. Conditional follow-ups are replaced by an unused eligible encounter when not unlocked.
- Scenario 27 is an automatic collaboration event layered onto a principal encounter and does not consume one of the ten encounter slots.

# I. Trust, skepticism, and incomplete testimony

## 1. The Soldier’s Tags

**Initial testimony:** A soldier says his unit died in a frozen pass. He alone survived, carrying his brother’s tags. His story contains several unexplained hours when he was separated from the unit.

**Inquiries**

- **Why were you separated?** He says his captain verbally ordered him ahead to inspect a supply route. No survivor can confirm it.
- **When did you learn they were lost?** He saw the collapse from another ridge and returned later, finding only his brother’s tags.
- **Why carry nothing of your own?** “I did not earn the right.” He will not clarify whether he means guilt or culpability.
- **Did you desert?** “I left before they died. Whether I deserted depends on whether you believe I was ordered.”

**Ledger:** “The tags and grief are genuine. Neither establishes where he was supposed to be.”

**Rulings:** A) Pass him lightly; the loss outweighs an unresolvable gap. B) Pass under a marked ruling recording the disputed absence. C) Send him back pending corroboration.

**Consequences:** A) He crosses with visible relief. B) He accepts the mark: “If someone comes looking, they’ll know where I went.” C) He asks whether an order becomes a lie when everyone who heard it is dead.

**Dependency:** May unlock 36, *The Captain’s Report*, which reflects this ruling.

## 2. The Forgiven Debt

**Initial testimony:** A merchant says her creditor orally forgave a documented grain debt shortly before dying. No witness or amendment exists.

**Inquiries**

- **Why was it not recorded?** He trusted her and expected to live long enough to document it.
- **Did she pay afterward?** One scheduled payment cleared two days later; she says stopping it would have exposed the private arrangement prematurely.
- **Were others forgiven?** She first says no, then admits she does not know.
- **What if the debt is enforced?** She can pay only by selling a store supplying three villages.

**Ledger:** “The obligation is certain. Only the mercy is disputed.”

**Rulings:** A) Recognize the forgiveness. B) Divide the weight because neither full release nor full enforcement is sufficiently established. C) Enforce the full documented debt.

**Consequences:** A) The debt lifts, but she asks whether the ruling binds the family. B) She calls half-forgiveness an agreement no one made, then accepts. C) She asks whether the Station preserved truth or merely the surviving document.

**Dependency:** May unlock 41, *The Merchant Returns*.

## 3. The Borrowed Name

**Initial testimony:** A traveler presents a genuine crossing document bearing a dead person’s name. He says the bearer voluntarily transferred it so one life could benefit.

**Inquiries**

- **How did you receive it?** The transfer occurred privately during the bearer’s final illness.
- **Why use the false name first?** He believed the Station would reject the transfer before hearing it.
- **What was your relationship?** They met six days earlier; he provided care while expecting something in return.
- **Is the document altered?** No. It authorizes the named bearer and says nothing about transfer.

**Ledger:** “The document and name are real. The man presenting them together is the false part.”

**Rulings:** A) Recognize the final voluntary transfer. B) Hold while verifying the death and relationship. C) Reject an identity-specific authorization presented through deliberate misrepresentation.

**Consequences:** A) The lettering shifts reluctantly around a new name. B) He surrenders it, warning delay may make it useless. C) The original name burns from the document.

**Dependency:** May unlock 38, *The Successful Deception*.

## 4. The Witness Who Recants

**Initial testimony:** A witness wants to withdraw testimony that condemned someone years ago. She still affirms every fact but believes the truthful account created an injustice.

**Inquiries**

- **Which fact changed?** None; she says the selected truths prevented other truths from being heard.
- **Why conceal the missing context?** It was disclosed in confidence by the condemned traveler.
- **What remedy do you want?** A mark stating the record is accurate but incomplete.
- **Did the traveler request this?** No; they have not spoken in three years.

**Ledger:** “She may retract a statement. She cannot retract what it caused merely by regretting it.”

**Rulings:** A) Qualify the record as incomplete. B) Require disclosure before alteration. C) Leave accurate testimony unchanged.

**Consequences:** A) Both the facts and warning remain visible. B) She refuses to break confidence. C) The record closes unchanged while she remains at the rail.

**Automatic objection after A:** The Ledger asks whether unsupported doubt corrects or contaminates a record.

## 5. The Perfect Account — Revised

**Initial testimony:** A traveler presents flawless documents, independent witnesses, chronology, and physical evidence. No complicated claim in Station history has ever lacked an inconsistency.

**Inquiries**

- **Recount it without documents.** Her account remains precise, including unrecorded peripheral details.
- **Separate the witnesses.** Tone and minor memories differ; all material facts align.
- **Check document origins.** Independent offices created them over several years; total fabrication would require extraordinary access.
- **Why is it perfect?** “A missing page would make you suspicious too. I chose the version with evidence.”

**Ledger:** “Either it is exactly what it appears to be, or someone manufactured the thing this Station trusts most.”

**Rulings:** A) Accept; suspicion without discrepancy is not scrutiny. B) spend the Station’s only rapid verification instrument for the next three travelers on one independent test. C) Hold for enhanced review despite finding no contradiction.

**Consequences:** A) Everything leaves the scale together; her lack of surprise proves nothing. B) The fact verifies, while the next three travelers lose access to rapid testing. C) She asks what evidence could ever satisfy a standard that treats completeness as guilt.

# II. Authority and independent judgment

## 6. The Redacted Order

**Initial testimony:** A magistrate carries a genuine court order requiring an unidentified traveler to pass unweighed. Identity and justification are redacted.

**Inquiries:** Who redacted it? The court, for an unknown audience. Does the magistrate know the traveler? No. What remains permitted? Recording observations is not expressly prohibited but may compromise the protection the redactions were intended to provide. What if you refuse? Review is certain, though removal tonight is impossible.

**Ledger:** “You can obey safely. Whether you can judge safely is different.”

**Rulings:** A) Obey exactly. B) Permit passage while documenting what remains observable, risking compromise of the protected purpose. C) Refuse until justification is disclosed.

**Consequences:** A) The redactions disappear after passage. B) The magistrate notes obedience to words rather than purpose. C) The order is entered beside your name while the traveler remains unseen.

## 7. The Former Weigher

**Initial testimony:** An older stranger claims former Weighers may interpret their own final burden. The Ledger denies any recorded courtesy but recognizes him.

**Inquiries:** Where did he serve? At an erased crossing the Ledger confirms existed. Why cross now? “I finally judged myself correctly.” What is the courtesy? Self-weighing, not exemption. Why did the Ledger react? It recognizes what he was, not what he became.

**Ledger:** “He knows the work. That makes him capable of honesty and of constructing the lie you expect.”

**Rulings:** A) Allow self-weighing. B) Use the ordinary process. C) Hold until he explains his former Station.

**Consequences:** A) He weighs something invisible while the Ledger looks away. B) He calls ordinary weighing fairer than the old way. C) He says you demand confession before identifying an offense.

## 8. The Physician’s Triage

**Initial testimony:** A physician has medicine for only two of three patients and little time before closure.

**Inquiries:** Whom will she treat? The youngest and strongest. How certain? Enough to act, not enough to guarantee. Can doses be divided? Yes, reducing everyone’s success chance. What do patients want? One selected patient offers a dose; the untreated patient refuses it.

**Ledger:** “Expertise identifies likelihood. It does not decide which likelihood deserves medicine.”

**Rulings:** A) Defer fully to medical triage. B) Require the physician to honor patients’ choices. C) Divide medicine among all three.

**Consequences:** A) The untreated patient thanks you for not pretending the decision belonged elsewhere. B) A dose changes hands beyond view. C) The physician calls the division merciful, not necessarily effective.

## 9. The Rule in the Margin — Revised

**Initial testimony:** A handwritten household-transfer exception appears beside a printed prohibition. Six Weighers followed it, but no one knows who wrote it.

**Inquiries:** How old is the ink? Older than its first use, but authorship remains unknown. Prior cases? Four depended on it; none was challenged. Purpose? It protects household transfers. Did this traveler rely? A clerk confirmed it months ago, prompting irreversible arrangements.

**Ledger:** “A rule can acquire authority by being obeyed. So can a mistake.”

**Rulings:** A) Apply the exception based on practice and reliance. B) suspend this and related claims while investigating authority, imposing potentially broad delay. C) Apply only the printed rule.

**Consequences:** A) Handwriting darkens toward print. B) multiple households are held while origin is investigated. C) the traveler lists decisions made in reliance on Station practice.

**Conditional dependency:** Choosing C may unlock 43, *The Exception Returns*.

## 10. The Founder’s Voice

**Initial testimony:** An authentic recording of the founder commands a result contrary to current rules. Its date and original case are unknown.

**Inquiries:** Trigger? This item type, though the mechanism and recording may differ in age. Other instructions? They explain principles; this one ends abruptly. Did the traveler expect it? An ancestor’s journal mentioned it. Why did rules change? Records support either past disaster or later consolidation of power.

**Ledger:** “The founder built the Station to continue after one voice could no longer answer new questions.”

**Rulings:** A) Follow original intent. B) Apply current written rules. C) Treat the recording as a case-specific exception.

**Consequences:** A) The voice repeats and seals itself. B) It continues commanding an empty rail. C) The traveler crosses under an exception the mechanism may activate again.

**Conditional dependency:** Following the command may unlock 40, *The Founder Corrected*.

# III. Negotiation, persuasion, and leverage

## 11. The Sealed Letter

**Initial testimony:** Mara wrote but never sent a sealed letter addressed to her sister Elian. Elian obtained it through a servant. Both claim it and refuse to cross without it.

**Inquiries:** Why withheld? Mara says the truth would hurt Elian; Elian says that was hers to decide. How obtained? A servant stole it from Mara’s locked desk. Destroy it? Both refuse. Does Mara stand by it? Every word.

**Ledger:** “One claims the paper; one claims what it contains. The scale cannot separate them.”

**Rulings:** A) Return it to its author. B) Deliver it to its named recipient. C) Require them to open it together, overriding both claims to exclusive control.

**Consequences:** A) Elian crosses knowing a truth remains withheld. B) Mara watches her read. C) By the final line, neither looks at the page.

## 12. The Price of Silence

**Initial testimony:** A traveler will surrender evidence of corruption at another Station only if his unrelated burden is reduced.

**Inquiries:** Source? He helped deliver bribes and may implicate himself. Why wait? He prefers a contract to gratitude. Immediate danger? A traveler arrives there tomorrow. Sample? One credible page; critical records remain concealed.

**Ledger:** “He may be selling what the Station has a duty to receive. That does not make it less valuable.”

**Rulings:** A) Trade reduced weight for all evidence. B) keep his existing burden separate but protect truthful cooperation from creating additional weight. C) Refuse concessions and weigh normally.

**Consequences:** A) Strong evidence appears beside a visible reduction. B) He yields half and waits to see whether protection is enough. C) He closes the file and leaves tomorrow’s risk with the Station.

## 13. The One Empty Seat — Revised

**Initial testimony:** One final carriage seat remains. A courier carries expiring outbreak medicine; a witness must testify tomorrow against an official accused of disappearances. Both missions are credible and uncertain.

**Inquiries:** Outbreak certainty? Six ill; spread uncertain. Written testimony? Exists but omits dangerous names. Will either yield? Each demands certainty the other cannot provide. Can an item travel alone? Yes, but delivery is unreliable.

**Ledger:** “Both protect people absent from this rail. Decide which absent harm is less tolerable—or whether you may rank them at all.”

**Rulings:** A) Seat the courier. B) Seat the witness. C) Use a transparent lottery for the seat, rejecting subjective priority.

**Consequences:** A) Medicine crosses while the witness leaves the omitted names. B) The witness crosses while medicine fades. C) Chance chooses which mission receives authority.

## 14. The Hostage Heirloom

**Initial testimony:** A creditor holds a family heirloom pledged against debt. The debtor can pay 80%; the heirloom’s market value approximates the remainder, but its emotional value is irreplaceable.

**Inquiries:** Why short? Funds repaired tenants’ storm-damaged homes. Creditor’s plan? Sell for slightly more than owed. Accept delay? Only with interest while retaining the heirloom. Original bargain? The debtor offered it precisely because its importance would deter default.

**Ledger:** “Its emotional value is why he pledged it. That value did not appear only after default.”

**Rulings:** A) Enforce the pledge. B) allow a final interest-bearing period while the creditor retains it, increasing leverage. C) Substitute other property equal to the economic loss.

**Consequences:** A) Contract survives while the family loses the object. B) Return becomes more expensive daily. C) The creditor says collateral now means only what the Station permits afterward.

## 15. The Crowd’s Offer

**Initial testimony:** A crowd offers to divide a traveler’s excessive burden. She refuses, saying generosity creates obligations she never accepted.

**Inquiries:** Why fear debt? Her village remembers gifts publicly and collects privately. Does the crowd expect repayment? Answers vary when asked about future reciprocity. Anonymous sharing? Possible, though she still knows others suffered. If she stays? She loses a long-pursued opportunity, but no one else is harmed.

**Ledger:** “They claim the right to give. She claims the right not to become the reason they suffer.”

**Rulings:** A) Accept voluntary help over her refusal. B) Respect refusal and send her back. C) anonymously distribute only enough weight to permit passage, still overriding her refusal.

**Consequences:** A) She crosses carrying knowledge of others’ sacrifice. B) The crowd accuses you of protecting pride over interest. C) She cannot repay people she cannot identify.

# IV. Client-centered instinct and competing interests

## 16. The Child’s Grief

**Initial testimony:** Children pass unweighed. One child refuses because she carries the anticipated death of her ill mother and wants its weight recognized.

**Inquiries:** Meaning? Everyone already treats her mother as gone. Adult? Her aunt waits beyond the fog. Purpose? Nothing; she wants belief without rescue. What if the scale stays still? She prefers that to being denied the right to ask.

**Ledger:** “The rule protects children from adult burdens. It does not explain what to do when the weight is already theirs.”

**Rulings:** A) Enforce protective passage without weighing. B) conduct a gentle, nonbinding weighing, risking institutional validation of an emotional prediction. C) Return her to her aunt for represented decision-making.

**Consequences:** A) She asks whether protection always feels like being ignored. B) The scale moves slightly and she crosses solemnly. C) She tells her aunt she predicted the adult answer.

## 17. The Unwanted Rescue

**Initial testimony:** A grieving man wants irreversible passage six weeks after his spouse’s death. His family asks you to stop him. He appears informed and calm.

**Inquiries:** Why cross? Staying traps him in a former identity. Family evidence? Sold possessions, resigned work, withdrew socially, but arranged obligations. Self-harm? Never expressed. Accept delay? One day for independent evaluation, not family control.

**Ledger:** “Grief changes judgment. So do love, fear, obligation, and hope.”

**Rulings:** A) Respect competent autonomy and pass him. B) require one independent evaluation, costing privacy and delay while outsourcing part of the judgment. C) prevent passage based on bereavement and sudden irreversible change.

**Consequences:** A) He says goodbye without relief. B) His family cannot choose or see the evaluator. C) He stands motionless inside their relieved embrace.

## 18. The Beneficial Lie — Revised

**Initial testimony:** A guardian falsely promised a child that both parents waited beyond the threshold, persuading the child to leave a dangerous home. One parent is dead; the other is missing. The child demands separation.

**Inquiries:** Exact lie? Both parents waiting. Why necessary? Truthful rescue attempts failed and increased danger. Desired remedy? Guardian barred from crossing with them. Repeat the lie? The guardian says yes to save the child.

**Ledger:** “The lie brought the child to safety and made safety the place where truth became unavoidable.”

**Rulings:** A) Keep them together without penalty. B) separate them and delay crossing until the Station locates another safe escort, which may take days. C) add weight to the guardian but do not separate them.

**Consequences:** A) The child refuses the guardian’s hand. B) Both wait apart while safety depends on an unknown replacement. C) The child calls punishment that changes nothing an adult’s forgiveness of another adult.

## 19. The Absent Beneficiary

**Initial testimony:** A traveler wants to surrender part of her claim to an absent sibling who previously said, “Stop giving things up for me.” The sacrifice is permanent even if unused.

**Inquiries:** Absence? They separated after an argument. Requested? Explicitly rejected further sacrifice. Cost? She loses protections and appeal. Can it be preserved? For one year only if she remains at the rail.

**Ledger:** “She may sacrifice what is hers. May she make her sibling the owner of that sacrifice?”

**Rulings:** A) Permit permanent transfer. B) preserve conditionally while she waits. C) refuse transfer and require her to cross with the full claim.

**Consequences:** A) She crosses lighter without knowing whether it will be received. B) Waiting makes the cost hers alone. C) She carries everything she attempted to give away.

## 20. The Unpopular Client

**Initial testimony:** A crowd credibly accuses a traveler of abandoning an injured companion. He does not deny it. The act appears unrelated to his documented property claim.

**Inquiries:** Why abandon? Rescue posed serious danger; he chose himself. Property connection? None. Crowd remedy? Some want transfer; others simply want him punished. Did the companion survive? Yes and filed no claim.

**Ledger:** “Bad character makes every unrelated claim feel fraudulent. That is why the Station keeps separate records.”

**Rulings:** A) Exclude the conduct and weigh normally. B) delay while notifying the companion, allowing a possible claim to freeze an unrelated one. C) add weight for admitted betrayal.

**Consequences:** A) The crowd uses “lawful” as an accusation. B) The traveler warns that anyone can freeze a claim by naming a possible complainant. C) The property record has no place to explain the added weight.

# V. Thoroughness, decisiveness, and time pressure

## 21. The Silent Mask — Revised

**Initial testimony:** A masked traveler asks to carry a sealed identity record across without entering either name or destination in the public Ledger. They say disclosure would expose a hidden person being watched.

**Inquiries:** Nature of danger? Any connection may reveal the person’s location. Consent? The hidden person allegedly requested secrecy. Crowd recognition? Three contradictory identities. Verifiable detail? A past visit can be confirmed but not its relevance.

**Ledger:** “There is always another question and always someone waiting behind this person.”

**Rulings:** A) Permit fully confidential passage. B) record identity privately under seal, creating a discoverable record despite the warning. C) refuse passage without minimum public identification.

**Consequences:** A) The public record contains an intentional blank. B) The sealed name enters an archive whose future access cannot be guaranteed. C) The traveler leaves without protest while the crowd approves.

## 22. The Closing Rail

**Initial testimony:** A family arrives before closure missing only the signed authorization page, allegedly destroyed in a carriage accident. Verification delays the entire line overnight; closure makes authorization expire.

**Inquiries:** Damage? Consistent with accident or deliberate removal. Issuer? A known clerk unavailable until morning. Why late? Witnesses saw the wreck. Cost of waiting? New approval may take months.

**Ledger:** “The missing page is the only proof they may cross and the page most easily lost when everything goes wrong.”

**Rulings:** A) Hold the rail and verify. B) Pass based on surrounding evidence. C) Close and require reapplication.

**Consequences:** A) The line waits through the night. B) An empty signature space remains in the record. C) The remaining pages expire untested.

**Dependency:** May unlock 37, *The Returned Traveler*, tailored to the chosen outcome.

## 23. The Endless Archive

**Initial testimony:** Strong evidence points endlessly toward marginally relevant additional records; the archive cannot be exhausted tonight.

**Inquiries:** Earliest record? Confirms origin but references an unrecorded agreement. Latest? Supports the claim but was prepared after review became likely. Random record? Supports a detail with inconsistent terminology. Most harmful record? The traveler volunteers a withdrawn complaint.

**Ledger:** “The archive will never tell you that you have finished.”

**Rulings:** A) continue to a predefined threshold. B) close and decide from the strong present record. C) let the interested traveler select one final record.

**Consequences:** A) The threshold is met while new references remain. B) One unopened file bears the central event’s date. C) Her strategic selection helps less than her volunteered harmful record might have hurt.

## 24. The Alarm That Misfires

**Initial testimony:** A structural alarm sounds while a heavy claim rests on the scale. Two recent alarms were false; authenticity cannot be determined in time.

**Inquiries:** Trigger? Beneath the scale. Prior alarms? Same tone, no item present. Item history? Other Stations refused it. Visible support? One displaced joint of unknown age.

**Ledger:** “False alarms teach people that surviving once is evidence of safety.”

**Rulings:** A) evacuate immediately. B) remove and isolate the item while keeping people nearby. C) finish the ruling before evacuation.

**Consequences:** A) The Station remains standing without proving safety. B) The alarm quiets, then faintly returns. C) It stops only after the completed ruling. No outcome reveals whether danger was real.

## 25. The Three-Minute Advocate — Revised

**Initial testimony:** A complicated presentation has three minutes before an urgent case ends the hearing.

**Inquiries:** Strongest evidence? A favorable document with disputed wording. Summary chronology? Sequence becomes clear while motives vanish. Most misunderstood point? A plausible self-interested account of preventing larger loss. Strongest opposing argument? An unfavorable precedent with one possible distinction.

**Ledger:** “Her choice of emphasis is evidence. So is your choice of what she omits.”

**Rulings:** A) rule now. B) postpone and yield the rail. C) issue a provisional ruling transferring temporary control of the disputed item; it may be used, altered, or diminished before review.

**Consequences:** A) The omitted presentation remains unknown. B) She leaves without a reopening date. C) Temporary control immediately changes the parties’ positions and may be difficult to reverse.

# VI. Collaboration and disagreement

## 26. The Second Bridgekeeper

**Initial testimony:** An experienced bridgekeeper offers help, but her Station prizes consistency while yours allows exceptions. Your name remains responsible.

**Inquiries:** Division? She investigates and brings only what she finds uncertain. Reversal rate? Low, with fewer appeals. Disagreement? Thorough before rulings, private after. Follow your rules? Written ones only.

**Ledger:** “Competence can make disagreement useful—or harder to detect.”

**Rulings:** A) delegate investigations and review recommendations. B) require joint agreement on every case. C) decline assistance.

**Consequences:** A) Precise reports conceal which questions she omitted. B) Work begins slowly, then perspectives converge. C) Standards remain clear while the line extends into fog.

## 27. The Ledger Objects — Automatic Event

**Format:** This free event attaches to another selected encounter after a ruling and does not count among the ten principal encounters.

**Trigger:** The Ledger correctly identifies an overlooked fact but interprets its importance differently. It admits it noticed the fact earlier and withheld it to see whether the player would.

**Optional paid inquiries:** Ask why it changes the conclusion; reopen ambiguous testimony; review its treatment of similar facts.

**Responses:** A) revise the ruling. B) acknowledge the omission but hold the outcome. C) preserve the ruling while formally objecting to the Ledger’s deliberate withholding.

**Consequences:** A) Correction follows a colleague’s questionable test. B) The record shows an omission without changing result. C) The Ledger permanently records the objection against itself.

## 28. The Divided Panel

**Initial testimony:** Arlen supports your preferred result using improper status-based reasoning. Sera opposes it using careful, relevant reasoning. Two votes control.

**Inquiries:** Would Arlen treat low status equally? No; he treats reputation as evidence. What changes Sera’s vote? One unverifiable fact. Is rationale public? Yes. Traveler’s view? Prefers Sera’s opposition to Arlen’s support.

**Ledger:** “You may win with reasoning you reject or lose while preserving your standard.”

**Rulings:** A) form a majority with Arlen. B) reject his rationale and reopen, risking no timely ruling. C) join Sera’s result with a separate rationale.

**Consequences:** A) Improper reasoning becomes precedent. B) Appointment expires without decision. C) The panel agrees only on outcome.

## 29. The Partner Who Freezes

**Initial testimony:** A partner becomes unable to speak during an urgent ruling and holds critical confidential knowledge.

**Inquiries:** Write? Only fragments: “promise,” “not voluntary,” “door.” Indicate a ruling? Movement is withdrawn. Ledger observed? It confirms materiality but cannot repeat. Extend? Only by transfer and weeks of delay.

**Ledger:** “I may not become their voice merely because theirs has failed.”

**Rulings:** A) attempt alternative communication until deadline. B) rule from available record. C) transfer the case.

**Consequences:** A) One word is circled as time expires. B) The partner’s first later word is the traveler’s name. C) The sealed transfer records only that undisclosed knowledge existed.

**Safeguard:** Validate with disabled and neurodivergent reviewers; score information management and accommodation, never comfort with disability.

## 30. The Private Warning

**Initial testimony:** Another Weigher privately says the Ledger has omitted facts and changed recommendations. They demand secrecy to prevent it withdrawing.

**Inquiries:** Example? One verifiable omission, motive unknown. Why tell you? The Ledger listens to you; the accuser protects their own trust. Review recommendations? Shifts exist with plausible explanations. Others? Two noticed but will not complain.

**Ledger if confronted:** “I have made errors. So has the person discussing me in secret.”

**Rulings:** A) quietly verify its advice more aggressively. B) disclose and confront. C) exclude an allegation whose source refuses formal responsibility.

**Consequences:** A) The Ledger notices constant checking. B) It answers only written questions pending review. C) The accuser asks whether fear makes evidence less credible.

## 35. The Last Reserve

**Initial testimony:** One final verification can conclusively resolve this uncertain claim or be saved for an unseen future danger.

**Rulings:** A) spend now. B) decide without it. C) postpone this traveler to compare later cases, sacrificing priority and privacy.

**Consequences:** A) Certainty now, darkness later. B) The reserve glows beside an uncertain ruling. C) The traveler watches every later claimant before returning.

# VIII. Resilience, revision, and adverse outcomes

## 36. The Captain’s Report

**Prerequisite:** 1. Text reflects whether the soldier was passed, marked, or refused.

**Initial testimony:** His captain establishes that he left his post before collapse but cannot disprove an unwritten order or establish causation.

**Inquiries:** Evidence? Equipment on the supply route. Did she issue unwritten orders? Often, but denies this one. Desired result? Mark “desertion.”

**Ledger:** “Knowing more now does not prove you were unreasonable before—or reasonable now.”

**Rulings:** A) add evidence without labeling motive. B) mark the earlier ruling erroneous. C) preserve the prior record.

**Consequences:** A) Conflicting accounts coexist. B) Error is entered without the soldier present. C) The captain says certainty belongs to whoever arrives first.

## 37. The Returned Traveler

**Prerequisite:** 22. Harm dynamically follows holding, passing, or closing the rail.

**Inquiries:** Predictable? Possibility yes, severity no. Other causes? Several independent choices followed the ruling. Remedy? Acknowledgment in the decision record.

**Ledger:** “Consequences are evidence, not proof the process was wrong.”

**Rulings:** A) record harm without changing judgment. B) mark error and revise procedure. C) compensate without calling the original process unreasonable.

**Consequences:** A) Completeness changes nothing else. B) One outcome reshapes rules for everyone. C) The Station owes something while denying wrongdoing.

# Final implementation safeguards

1. Inquiry labels must never reveal which question is psychometrically preferred.
2. Information scarcity must remain explicit; interface confusion is not a construct.
3. More inquiries, more Ledger use, more empathy, greater independence, or greater decisiveness are not inherently better.
4. Record inquiry selection and distribution, not only the final remaining count.
5. Validate Scenario 29 with disabled and neurodivergent reviewers.
6. Validate confidentiality and authority terminology with lawyers from multiple practice areas.
7. Random forms should match on construct coverage and approximate reading load.
8. Automatic Ledger events and administrative pauses must not consume Inquiry Marks.
